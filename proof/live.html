Step 4 · The live desk

The final's clock moves by itself: a price request must be answered within 4.5 real seconds, and the system must survive restarts and network trouble while the week goes on. The live desk follows the event stream, keeps the step 3 policy, and rebuilds itself from the world's records whenever it starts. To test it before the test final, a pacer ran a practice world from Thursday 00:00 on the final's clock at double speed (quote windows of 2.25 s), and the desk was killed four minutes in and restarted five seconds later.

First rehearsal: the desk joined before the world reached Thursday and spent 74 s on Monday to Wednesday's events, one network call per quote, so 121 Thursday quotes arrived after their windows. Fix: quotes go out from their own thread and connection, batched while a call is in flight, and a quote whose window has closed is skipped.

Test final 1 (Saturday 14:00, the real clock)

The desk answered all 417 requests in time on the real clock (quote latency 0.016 s median, 0.98 s worst). At Thursday 09:38 a post lost its connection after it was sent; the desk did not resend it blindly, rejoined in 3.3 s, saw from the snapshot that the gig did not exist and posted it once. It finished ranked at 100% on time, but won only 12 of 417 requests: the top ranked teams won 135-167 orders and €3,100-4,000. The fixed 1.5× list price was too high for this world (49 of 55 ranked teams earned more). The infrastructure works; the price must adapt to each world. That is the next step.

What protects the desk in the final

  • Restart at any time. On start the desk reads the snapshot and the whole event log and rebuilds its orders and gigs (the kit's rejoin), then follows the stream from the last event.
  • Stream supervisor. Any failure (refused handshake, close 1013, network) reconnects from the last sequence number; a skipped number is filled from GET /events.
  • No double sends. A POST whose answer is lost is never sent again blindly (the kit client's resend applied requests twice on a flaky network).
  • No ended final. GET /v1/final keeps returning the last final after it ends; the desk joins only a world whose clock is before its end.
  • No invalid batch. Every action is checked against the server's schema; an invalid one is dropped and logged instead of failing its whole batch.
