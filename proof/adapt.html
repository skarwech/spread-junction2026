Step 5 · A price that adapts to each world

In test final 1 the desk answered every request in time but won only 12 orders: a fixed 1.5× list was too dear for that world, while the competitor's level differs from world to world (1.26-1.54× list). The adaptive price starts at 1.5× and steers the multiplier so that about 40% of real quotes win. It moves at most 4% per update, only every 30 world minutes and only after 5 new outcomes (so a bulk order cannot swing it and it does not drift overnight). It never quotes below the request's typical fee × 1.15, and it pushes the price up instead when more than 80 orders were won in the last 24 hours. It is a pure function of events, so the desk rebuilds it exactly after a restart.

The multiplier in each world (policy C)

Each world settles at its own price: where the competitor is dear the desk stays high, where it is cheap it walks down toward list.

What was decided

  • C is adopted for test final 2 and the final: ranked everywhere, the best worst week, and the most margin among the policies ranked on every week.
  • E is rejected: a looser slack filter earns more but misses the gate on hold-5 (87.8%).
  • The trust markup is a trade, not a gain: at the 30% target it cost margin (B +€64,247.38 with it, D +€65,262.65 without) but raised the worst week (95.0% against 93.9%). It is kept in C for that buffer; C without it was not tested.
  • The earlier hold-out failures were artefacts: hold-2 and hold-5 are ranked under the fixed price too (94.2%, 93.3%); they had failed only while the kit client sent requests twice on a flaky network.
